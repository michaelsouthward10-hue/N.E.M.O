NORSE MYTHOLOGY · THE OPEN SEA

Thor and
the Midgard Serpent.

A fishing voyage, an ox-head for bait, and the moment Thor draws the world-serpent from the deep.

A Mythos retelling · 3 minute read

In Snorri Sturluson’s prose account, Thor sets out in the shape of a young traveller and stays with the giant Hymir. At dawn they row far beyond Hymir’s usual fishing grounds. Thor takes an ox-head for bait and casts his line into the deep.[1]

The Midgard Serpent, Jörmungandr, seizes the bait. Its pull jolts the boat; Thor braces his feet against the sea-bed and hauls the huge serpent up to the gunwale. For a moment, god and serpent stare at one another across the rocking boat.[1]

Thor reaches for Mjölnir. In Snorri’s telling, Hymir panics and cuts the line, sending the serpent back beneath the waves. Snorri then mentions another version in which Thor strikes its head with the hammer—but says he believes the serpent still lives in the sea.[1]

The poem Hymiskviða gives the encounter in verse: Thor draws the serpent up and strikes it with his hammer; the sea and earth shudder, and the serpent sinks back into the ocean. The poem does not plainly say whether the blow kills it.[2]

Two tellings, different endings.

The prose account describes Hymir cutting the line and preserves a disagreement over whether Thor’s hammer struck. Hymiskviða makes the hammer-blow part of the scene, then leaves the serpent sinking into the sea. A modern scholarly overview traces this encounter across poems, Snorri’s account, and Viking Age images—and notes that the serpent’s fate varies between tellings.[3]
